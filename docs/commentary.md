# Commentary: preserving the particular thing

The useful question raised by this example is how a reconstruction preserves the features that make a particular image mean what it does. A recognisable collection of objects can coexist with a consequential error in their relationship. A head, a power strip and a returning loop of cable are enough for broad resemblance. Where the plug goes determines the depicted assembly.

## Fidelity is a selection problem

The caricature analogy is helpful. Exaggerating a few distinguishing features can preserve a person's identity. Omitting those features can produce a generic face; adding plausible but incorrect features can produce a different one. Greater detail alone does not guarantee greater fidelity. The important question is which information survives the transformation.

The same principle applies to a reconstruction of an object. Some freedom in unseen depth, surface treatment and resolution is unavoidable. That freedom does not by itself justify changing a distinguishing connection. A small junction can carry substantial meaning while contributing little to the overall silhouette.

This motivates assessment of features and relationships retained, omitted or invented. A broad resemblance score can hide precisely the errors the study needs to expose.

## Uncertainty is an observation about the response

Early interpretation of the pilot treated the models' uncertainty about the plug attachment as support for calling the drawing ambiguous. That inference was too strong. The observations establish that the model qualified its reading; they do not independently establish that the image lacks sufficient clues.

The drawing supplies a distinct plug body, a cable originating at the strip, a socket-bearing face, and contours and overlaps around the returning plug. Overlooking or incorrectly weighting those cues could produce both an incorrect reconstruction and a later explanation that the source was unclear. The same omission could recur when the model compares the original and its replica.

Agreement between a generator and a critic using the same model therefore requires scrutiny. They may share an incomplete reading. The model's narrative should be checked against the source and geometry rather than used to define the ground truth.

Historical protocols are preserved as written. Current assessments explicitly distinguish reported uncertainty from a finding about source sufficiency.

## A visible socket is not necessarily a receptacle

The volumetric audit adds a further distinction. In the retained image reconstructions, dark slots and round marks are often represented by positive surface geometry. That can resemble a socket in a coloured render while leaving solid plastic where the prongs should go. Even the two replicas with the intended depicted attachment have substantial prong/plastic intersections.

This defect can be measured without asking a model what the image means. Exact mesh intersections, calibrated with known counterexamples, show the collision in the retained pose. Source inspection then shows that the housing has no corresponding cavities.

The earlier visual task permitted overlapping solids and excluded mechanical mating. Its attachment score remains a score of depicted placement. The functional audit adds another requirement; it does not silently change the earlier contract.

## Capability and spontaneous preservation differ

Both text-only controls build genuine, closed-mesh cavities when given exact functional requirements. Both pass independent bounds, volume and clearance checks. This demonstrates available construction capability in those examples. It leaves open why the image task produced surface symbolism instead.

Several explanations remain possible: selecting broad appearance over distinguishing geometry, failing to carry an inferred relationship into coordinate constraints, or checking against an incomplete representation. These are hypotheses about the workflow. The evidence does not identify a proprietary internal representation or prove a general lack of spatial understanding.

The general fidelity/checking prompt also did not produce a seated receiving socket in either trial. That is a result for one wording and a small sample, rather than a conclusion that additional checking is ineffective. The earlier explicit attachment prompt changed interpretation, attention and geometry together, so its success cannot isolate those contributions.

## The next discriminating comparison

Keep the drawing and reconstruction task fixed, and add a functional-geometry requirement without specifying where the plug should be placed. Assess the attachment and receiving voids independently. This can reveal whether a requirement to preserve physical affordances changes the geometry, the selected connection, both or neither.

Subsequent work should test rendered feedback and unfamiliar assemblies with independently specified distinguishing features. A complete programme needs evidence that checks can reject plausible substitutes, including ones a model describes confidently. It should retain counterexamples and uncertainty in its conclusions while preserving concrete fidelity criteria for the artefacts themselves.
